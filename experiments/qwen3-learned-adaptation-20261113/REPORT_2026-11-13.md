# Qwen3-0.6B: learning to construct a working representation and decide its computation horizon

**Executed local CPU study — 2026-11-13. Exploratory, not a preregistered or blind benchmark study.**

## Research question

Can a small system connected to a pretrained model **learn both what state to retain and when to reuse a computation** from final outcomes, instead of receiving a two-hop graph solution or supervised edit traces? Can it then continue beyond the horizons seen in training?

## Mechanism tested

A frozen pretrained Qwen3-0.6B supplies 1,024-dimensional sentence and question representations. An external **301,551-parameter** trainable computation learns eight anonymous latent node addresses, directed relational bindings, a graph workspace, a question-relative starting/target state, and a **shared continue/STOP gate**. A single graph traversal is reused recursively. The gate receives the evolving latent workspace and the question; it decides when to finish, including after a fourth traversal never seen as a training target. The underlying *form of the graph, traversal operator, and reverse compass relation* are architectural priors deliberately provided by the researchers. This is **not** automatic invention of a tensor operation from scratch.

The graph constructor and stopping gate start from random initialization in the *joint training* experiments. No intermediate graphs, transition labels, manually specified room-to-node assignments, or supporting-fact annotations are provided. Supervision is final path answers only. Since answer strings themselves contain one, two, or three direction tokens, they **implicitly disclose path length**; this should not be misrepresented as completely unsupervised operation selection.

## Data and protocol

Source: original Facebook bAbI Task 19 `en-10k`, 10,000 training stories and 1,000 official test stories. The original test is 2-hop questions only. To get a range of computational horizons, we deterministically generate one shortest-path query per story for each path length 1, 2, and 3, plus one 4-hop query from each of the 628 test stories that have such a path. A classical breadth-first search over the original five explicit relations produces the answer **for labeling/evaluation only**; it is never shown as intermediate supervision. We train on 9,000 stories (27,000 generated final-answer examples), validate on 1,000 disjoint train stories (3,000 examples), and evaluate on 1,000 disjoint original test stories (3,000 examples). Four-hop questions are held out entirely from training. All 120 sentence types and 30 question templates occur among training text types; results test compositional/horizon transfer, **not unseen vocabulary or arbitrary new tasks**.

Training uses Qwen3’s frozen extracted features and unsupervised whitening, AdamW, and two CPU threads. The staged curriculum is 20 epochs of one-hop final-answer questions, 28 epochs of one/two-hop questions, then 67 epochs of one/two/three-hop questions. Every stage uses final answers only; no new neural modules, intermediate solution labels, or expert traces are introduced. Validation selects checkpoints. No original Qwen3 pretrained weights are updated in this joint model.

## Measured results

| Trained state construction + STOP | 1–3-hop test, 3,000 | Unseen 4-hop, 628 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Joint training, no curriculum, seed 61425 | 1,039 | 1 |
| Joint training + curriculum, seed 61425 | 2,444 | 325 |
| Joint training + curriculum, seed 61426 | **2,958** | **616** |
| Joint training + curriculum, seed 61427 | 1,120 | 2 |
| Curriculum with Qwen-feature vectors permuted among sentences, seed 61425 | 799 | 0 |
| Same permuted-feature control, seed 61426 | 2,657 | 476 |
| Classical exact graph/BFS reference | 3,000 | 628 |

Models in the matched seed 61426 comparison were trained with the same curriculum and task split; a consistent random permutation of all 150 existing Qwen feature vectors preserves the aggregate geometry but destroys their assignment to sentence meanings. On the same 3,000 held-out questions, the original-feature checkpoint scored 2,958 and the permuted-feature checkpoint 2,657 (paired exclusive wins 343 vs 42, exact two-sided binomial p=7.84e-60; story-bootstrap gain 95% interval **8.7–11.4 percentage points**). On the 628 unseen four-hop questions the same two models scored 616 and 476 (paired exclusive wins 152 vs 12, p=4.85e-32; bootstrap gain **18.8–26.0 points**). These p-values condition on the two selected seeds and the repeatedly investigated benchmark; they do not eliminate significant initialization and researcher-selection uncertainty. The other seeds show major optimization failure, so no stable Qwen-specific advantage has been established across independently repeated trainings.

**Causal intervention**, seed 61426 model evaluated on the same seed 61425 task sample: with its current story’s graph, 2,955/3,000 correct; replacing graph inputs with the preceding story’s events, 110/3,000. For unseen four-hop questions: **618/628 → 3/628**. A constant wrong story similarly yields 157/3,000 and 3/628. This confirms the model uses the constructed per-story memory and is not simply mapping question text to final answers.

An exploratory posthoc node-identity audit found that the best checkpoint’s endpoint addresses mapped to a single stable room-to-node lookup on just 124/240 source/target occurrences. The representation may be distributed or role-dependent; solving the task does not establish a clean human-interpretable node ontology. Room labels were used *only* for this diagnostic, never as training targets.

## Frozen-graph and native-Qwen ablations

A simpler **Qwen-conditioned STOP controller**, trained using a previously successful fixed graph checkpoint rather than learning the graph, scored **611, 610, 609 / 628** on four-hop paths across three controller seeds. A controller without latent graph feedback scored **0/628** in each seed; a graph-state-only controller scored **609/628**. Therefore graph state is critical, but Qwen question features supplied little measurable extra information in this setting. A **nonlearned reachability rule** scored **622–623/628**, outperforming these trained stopping rules on the new horizon; the learned version is not globally optimal.

We also actually trained rank-4 low-rank updates inside Qwen3’s own **final pretrained transformer decoder block** (attention output and MLP output) with continuous graph-state inputs, while freezing the other 27 layers. This adds 96,385 trainable LoRA/readout/normalization parameters. Complete path accuracy was **2,925/3,000 on the trained horizons and 610/628 on four-hop tests**. An identically trained model with the same pretrained decoder block **entirely frozen** and only its readout trained scored **2,927/3,000 and 610/628**. Thus the Qwen3 weight update occurred, but **did not yield an improvement**. This late-layer adapter setup is not equivalent to full-model LoRA fine-tuning of token-level Qwen3.

## Scope of the positive finding

A small pretrained-model-connected system can, in some seeds, learn from final outcomes to **build a useful continuous representation and decide when to reuse the same operation**. It can transfer a learned read–rewrite–continue decision to one extra computation step. No manual intermediate state or expert operation-selection labels were necessary. In the better seed, this was achieved with ~301K trainable parameters on local CPU.

This is **not proof of autonomous primitive discovery**: graph structure, directional inverse and traversal were still provided as operators; only their learned bindings, use, and stopping policy were trained. Also, the learned system still loses to an exact classical solver; controls with arbitrary sentence IDs sometimes learn very well; and training is unstable. The user’s broader hypothesis that all improvements are latent in pretrained weights remains a motivation, not a theorem established by these data.

## Reproducibility and next hard gate

The companion ZIP contains exact executed Python files, text datasets, frozen Qwen feature vectors, lightweight checkpoints, train logs, intermediate and paired test predictions, and causal ablations. It deliberately excludes the 1.5 GB official Qwen3-0.6B model weights, which must be obtained independently for the native LoRA test. Scripts were executed with their original absolute path `/mnt/data/qwen_next/`; recreate that directory or update `P` in copies for portability.

The next gate is **operation invention rather than operation reuse**: expose the model to a neutral, fixed budget of candidate latent computations (e.g., composition, reversal, projection, abstention), have it *form* or parameterize a missing computation from final outcomes in several unrelated problem families, and compare against equal-compute classical search and operation libraries under a truly untouched test protocol. Any new claims must distinguish learned useful new structure from a supplied graph compiler and avoid choosing only successful seeds.
